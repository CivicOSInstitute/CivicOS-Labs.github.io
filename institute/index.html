Formation of the CivicOS Institute

Our legal structure, our commitment, and the road ahead.

CivicOS Labs, LLC exists for one reason: to facilitate the formation of the CivicOS Institute, a planned nonprofit organization dedicated to open civic infrastructure, government accountability, and transparent public procurement.

This page explains how our legal structure works, why it is set up this way, and what comes next. We are sharing this publicly because transparency is not something we will ask of others and withhold ourselves.

Why an LLC First?

Forming a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization takes time — typically 6 to 12 months from filing to IRS determination, with legal and filing costs that can run several thousand dollars. In that gap, an operating entity is needed to:

  • Accept resources — people who believe in the mission can support it now, not after a government determination letter arrives.
  • Enter contracts — developers, vendors, and service providers need a legal counterparty.
  • Hold intellectual property — source code, trademarks, and research produced during formation should be owned by the mission, not by any individual.
  • Build the evidence base — public records, research, and tooling that will become the Institute's initial body of work.

The LLC is a standard pre-formation vehicle. It is not an end-run around nonprofit governance, nor is it a permanent choice. It is a practical step — designed to preserve momentum during a necessarily slow legal process.

The Commitment

Once the CivicOS Institute is fully formed as a recognized tax-exempt nonprofit and an independent board is seated, the following will happen:

  1. CivicOS Labs, LLC will be transferred to become a wholly owned subsidiary of the CivicOS Institute.
  2. The Institute's independent board will have full governance authority over the LLC and all of its assets.
  3. All intellectual property, contracts, and assets held by the LLC will belong to the nonprofit.
  4. The Institute may continue using the LLC as an operational subsidiary, dissolve it, or restructure it — at the board's sole discretion.

Until transfer, the LLC operates under a governance commitment equivalent to a nonprofit: mission-locked, nonpartisan, and accountable to the public interest. No founder has personal ownership rights to the organization's intellectual property or assets beyond standard compensation for work performed.

Timeline

CivicOS Institute has not yet been formed. The current timeline is:

  • May 2026: Legal research and counsel engagement for nonprofit formation. Charitable solicitation review underway.
  • Mid-2026: Articles of incorporation and IRS Form 1023 preparation.
  • Late 2026 / Early 2027: Expected 501(c)(3) determination, independent board seating, and LLC transfer.

These dates are estimates. The legal process will not be rushed, and the board will not be seated until the governance structure is strong enough to serve the mission independently.

Why Not Wait Until the Nonprofit Exists?

Because the work should not wait for a letter from the IRS.

Public procurement data grows stale. FOIA requests have statutory deadlines. School boards and city councils make decisions every week that affect millions of people. A 12-month formation window should not mean 12 months of silence.

The LLC allows us to build the evidence base, develop the tools, and demonstrate the method so that the CivicOS Institute launches with a body of work — not just a promise of work to come.

What This Means for Supporters

Important: This is not legal, tax, or financial advice.

Contributions to CivicOS Labs, LLC are not tax-deductible as charitable donations. Once the CivicOS Institute is formed and the LLC is transferred, prior supporters will have no special governance rights unless formally appointed to the board. We encourage every potential supporter to consult their own legal and tax advisors.

Supporters joining before the Institute is formed are helping build the institution itself — funding the research, tools, and operational capacity that will become the Institute's initial endowment of work. We are grateful for that trust and will earn it with transparency, discipline, and results.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is CivicOS Labs a nonprofit?
No, not yet. CivicOS Labs is currently a Florida limited liability company. The CivicOS Institute is the planned nonprofit, but it has not been formed or received tax-exempt determination from the IRS.
Are contributions tax-deductible?
No. Contributions to the LLC are not tax-deductible as charitable donations. We will clearly communicate all changes in tax treatment if and when the Institute is formed and contributions become deductible.
Could the LLC decide not to transfer?
No. The LLC's founding documents and governance commitments require transfer to a qualified nonprofit once the Institute is formed and an independent board is seated. This is not a discretionary decision.
Who controls the LLC now?
The LLC is managed by its founder, Nick Cerbone, who serves as director during the formation phase. All material decisions — including financial commitments, intellectual property assignments, and governance changes — are documented and will be fully transparent to the Institute's future board.
Why two websites — civicoslabs.com and civicos-institute.org?
civicoslabs.com is the Labs LLC's operational site, where current work, products, and formation updates live. civicos-institute.org is reserved for the future Institute. Once the nonprofit is formed, civicos-institute.org will become the primary public face, and civicoslabs.com will direct there.
What happens if the Institute is never formed?
If the Institute cannot be formed — due to legal, financial, or other barriers — the LLC's assets will be transferred to another qualified nonprofit with a compatible mission, or dissolved and distributed per Florida law. The mission assets will not revert to private ownership.
